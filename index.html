Summarizing the transcript trades a ledger for a quote

Context compression sells itself as cleanup, but run it as a purchase order. Take a forty turn engineering session with a 3,000 token system kit and 1,200 tokens of new work per turn. Full resend pricing turns the crank: the bill for that session is 1,056,000 input tokens, $3.17 at $3.00 a million, and the last turn alone re-reads 49,800 tokens because the whole history rides along every time.

Compress by replacing the history with a 500 token summary plus a ten turn working window and the steady turn reads 15,500 tokens, 620,000 for the session, $1.86. That is a 41 percent cut, and $1.31 is what the summary actually bought. The price is not on the invoice: every number that only ever existed inside a dropped turn, the chosen row counts, the exact error text, the agreed threshold, is gone the moment the summary lands. The transcript could be re-derived from artifacts; the summary cannot be re-derived at all.

So the compression decision is a records policy, not a cleanup step. Sessions where the turns are receipts, support escalations, incident timelines, pricing decisions, are the ones where 41 percent savings is the expensive side of the trade. Sessions where the turns are exploration already superseded by code or docs are where the same cut is free money. The audit question is never whether the summary is shorter. It is what number you would need in six months that only the transcript holds.
